uIDL — User Identification Digital Landing

Grammar for Safe Identity Presence

uIDL is the neutral identity envelope and digital landing layer for CyberCity.

uIDL defines the smallest possible digital identity presence that can enter a subsystem, place, or interaction surface without changing the sovereignty, behavior, or continuity of that environment.

uIDL is a grammar for safe identity presence.

It defines how identity presence can be structurally represented without interpreting identity.

---

Purpose

uIDL exists to solve one core problem:

«How can identity appear inside a digital environment without becoming a profile, a prediction system, or an authority mechanism?»

uIDL provides a neutral landing format where identity can exist as presence only.

It expresses:

- existence
- presence
- availability

It does not express:

- meaning
- metadata
- authority
- behavior
- history

---

Core Doctrine

Identity Is Presence, Not Meaning

uIDL represents presence without interpretation.

A uIDL artifact does not answer:

- Who someone is
- What someone likes
- Where someone has been
- What someone will do
- What someone is worth

Those are separate layers and are never required for basic presence.

---

Non-Interference Doctrine

uIDL cannot:

- mutate place
- alter continuity
- gain authority
- activate behavior
- influence sovereignty
- create hidden relationships

Identity presence must remain structurally neutral.

---

Dewey Authority Model

uIDL is a definition authority, not a storage authority.

uIDL catalogs:

- identity description primitives
- neutral envelopes
- presence formats
- structural validation rules

uIDL does not store:

- identity instances
- profiles
- personal metadata
- behavioral history

uIDL defines the language of identity presence without becoming the owner of identity.

---

Identity Pipeline

uIDL → CyberCity → Palm Display

Presence → Context → Interaction

uIDL

Provides neutral identity presence.

CyberCity

Provides place context.

CyberCity defines where identity is present, but never defines who identity is.

Palm Display

Provides the interaction surface.

Palm Display presents interaction without mutating identity.

---

Identity Description Primitives

Identity primitives are the smallest structural units of identity presence.

They contain only what is necessary to represent existence.

They do not contain:

- personality
- preferences
- permissions
- history
- behavioral signals

---

Neutral Envelopes

Neutral envelopes carry identity presence across boundaries.

They preserve structure without adding interpretation.

A neutral envelope does not create:

- ownership
- authority
- trust score
- behavior model

---

Presence Formats

Presence formats describe how identity is present.

Examples:

- AVAILABLE
- PRESENT

Presence state is not intent.

Presence ≠ Intent

A presence artifact existing near a place does not automatically trigger action.

---

Validation Rules

Future validation rules exist only to preserve structural compliance.

They validate:

- format integrity
- doctrine compliance
- boundary safety

They do not evaluate:

- identity value
- behavior
- intent
- personality

---

uIDL Security Model

uIDL artifacts are designed to remain:

- immutable
- non-semantic
- non-authoritative
- non-interpreted

Artifacts are frozen after creation.

No mutation.
No enrichment.
No correlation.

---

Architectural Principle

The system must preserve this separation:

Identity
    ↓
Presence

Place
    ↓
Context

Intent
    ↓
Interaction

No layer should collapse these boundaries.

---

Summary

uIDL is the neutral grammar for safe identity presence.

It allows digital identity to enter an environment without:

- being profiled
- being interpreted
- being enriched
- being granted authority
- changing the environment it enters

uIDL provides the foundation for identity safety in CyberCity by allowing presence without control.
